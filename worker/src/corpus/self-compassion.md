# Self-compassion

## A friend's voice

Think of a friend who told you the thought you are having right now. What would you say to them? Most
people answer with more room, more context, and less blame than they give themselves. Self-compassion
is not letting yourself off the hook. It is speaking to yourself with the same accuracy you would
offer someone you respect. Try writing one sentence in the second person, as if it were addressed to
that friend.

## Common humanity

A hard week can feel like evidence that you are uniquely behind. In fact, struggling with deadlines,
exams, sleep, and self-doubt is a shared human experience, not a private defect. Remembering that
does not erase the difficulty. It changes the meaning of the difficulty from "something is wrong with
me" to "this is a hard thing that people go through". That shift often makes it easier to ask for
help.

## One kind act

Self-compassion becomes more believable when it has a behaviour attached. Drink water, eat something
simple, step outside for five minutes, or send one honest message to someone safe. Choose something
small enough that you will actually do it today. The act is not a reward for productivity. It is a
signal to yourself that your needs count while the problem is still being solved.
