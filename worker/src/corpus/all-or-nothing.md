# All-or-nothing thinking

<!-- keywords: all-or-nothing, failure, gagal, perfect, sempurna, perfectionism, not good enough, tidak cukup, always, never, selalu, tidak pernah -->

## What it sounds like

All-or-nothing thinking turns a complicated result into a single verdict. It sounds like "I will fail
no matter what", "I always mess this up", or "if it is not perfect it is worthless". The giveaway is
an absolute word doing work that the evidence cannot support: always, never, everything, nothing,
everyone. Spotting the absolute word is the first step, not because the feeling is false, but because
the sentence is narrower than the situation.

## Evidence from the week

Look for one concrete counter-example from the last seven days. A task you finished after a bad
night. A message you answered. A paper you passed when you expected to fail. The point is not to
argue yourself into feeling good; it is to give the verdict one more piece of evidence to account
for. One counter-example is enough to make "always" inaccurate.

## A fairer sentence

A fairer sentence keeps what is true and drops the absolute. "This paper was hard and I am worried
about the result" is accurate and still serious. "I am a failure" is a conclusion that goes beyond
the paper. You do not have to replace the thought with a positive one. Accurate is enough, and
accurate is usually easier to believe.
