# Stress

## What stress does to a body

Stress is the body's response to a demand it thinks is bigger than its resources. The heart speeds up,
shoulders tighten, breathing gets shallow, and attention narrows. These are ordinary signals that the
body is preparing to meet something, not proof that something is wrong with you. Naming the signal
can make it easier to choose a next step instead of fighting the feeling.

## Exam pressure

An exam concentrates attention on one performance and makes it feel like the whole story. A single
paper measures a slice of knowledge on one day, not the whole degree, the whole year, or the whole
person. When the pressure spikes, separate the event ("this exam is hard") from the verdict ("I am
not enough"). The first can be worked with. The second is a judgement the exam cannot actually make.

## A smaller reset

When the load is high, shrink the next step until it fits the next ten minutes. Open one page, write
one message, review one question. A smaller step is not a smaller ambition; it is a way to keep
moving while the bigger pressure is still there. After the step, notice what changed, even slightly,
and choose the next small step from there.
