## The two-minute start

The hardest part of a task is often the transition into it. Give yourself a two-minute version: open
the document, write the first line, or read the first page. You are allowed to stop after two
minutes. Starting is a separate skill from finishing, and a small start is easier to repeat than a
large promise. If the two minutes turn into ten, that is a bonus, not the requirement.

## Make the step concrete

A vague step ("work on the assignment") gives you nothing to begin. A concrete step names an action,
a place, and a duration: "open the assignment brief at the kitchen table for ten minutes". The
smaller and more specific the step, the less room there is for negotiation. Write it down in the form
you would give a friend, then do only that.

## When the task is vague

If you do not know what the next step is, make the next step finding out. Read the instructions,
list three questions, or ask one person for the part you are missing. Not knowing is a normal stage
of the work, not a verdict about your ability. The goal is to leave the task with one more piece of
information than you had before, even if the task itself is not finished.
