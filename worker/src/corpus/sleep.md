# Sleep

## Wind-down

Sleep starts before you lie down. A short wind-down gives the body a repeated signal that the day is
ending: dim the lights, put the phone out of reach, and do one low-effort thing such as stretching,
reading, or washing up. The aim is not a perfect routine. It is a predictable edge between the day
and the night, so the mind has less to carry into bed.

## Late-night scrolling

Scrolling at night can feel like the only quiet time in the day. It also keeps attention active and
makes the transition to sleep harder. If stopping altogether feels unrealistic, choose a stopping
point before you start: one episode, one chapter, or a timer. Put the phone on the other side of the
room when the stopping point arrives. The goal is to reduce the decision at the moment when it is
hardest to make.

## Morning light

Morning light helps set the rhythm for the following night. If you can, step outside or sit near a
window for a few minutes after waking. Keep the wake time roughly stable, even after a poor night.
A consistent morning anchor is often more useful than trying to force an early bedtime. If sleep
problems continue for weeks and affect your days, talk to a health professional.
